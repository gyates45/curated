Collecting is easy and feels productive; it isn't the value. A knowledge
base — a personal one like this vault, or a law firm's document vault behind
an AI tool — is only as good as what's been *excluded* from it. Dump
everything in and the signal drowns: the AI can't tell your best material
from the draft that went badly, and neither can future-you.

The mechanism: retrieval quality degrades with corpus noise faster than it
improves with corpus size. So the leverage is in the boring governance —
finals only, a named owner, a recurring pruning slot, superseded material out
the same day it's replaced. A vault is a garden, not a landfill.

The boundary: this applies to *knowledge* stores, not capture funnels. The
Reader queue and inbox are allowed to be messy — that's what triage is for.
Curate at the gate between capture and canon, not at capture time.

**So what:** it's the same argument to clients ("your AI vault needs a
curator") and to this system's own weekly review — which is why the weekly
review is non-negotiable and capped at 30 minutes.
